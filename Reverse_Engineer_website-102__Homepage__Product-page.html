Field notes · website 102 · sheet a

Spacing, headings, and the anatomy of a real store

Sheet 00 used a made-up brand so we could point anywhere. This one uses a real, live, Indian store — beminimalist.co — read directly off the page. Every structure, price and heading below was on their site when these notes were written. Open the site in a second tab and follow along.

Real site · read live · open it alongside

Why Minimalist is the right site to learn on

Founded 2020 in Jaipur, built on radical ingredient transparency — they print the exact concentration on the front of the bottle. That obsession shows up in the interface: very little decoration, very few colours, and a heavy reliance on space and hierarchy to organise a lot of technical information. When a site removes decoration, spacing becomes the only thing holding it together. That makes it the perfect place to learn margin, padding and headings.

The screens in this notebook are teaching reconstructions built from the live page structure — same layout logic, same content shape, my own CSS. Not their code, not their images, not their photography.

Part one — the hero banner
01 / purpose

The hero has one job, and it isn't looking nice

A hero banner exists to answer three questions in about two seconds: what is this, why should I care, where do I click. If a hero doesn't answer all three, it's a decoration with a mortgage — it's occupying the most expensive space on the site.

What the real site actually does — and the trade-off

Minimalist's live homepage does not build its hero out of HTML text like the reconstruction above. It stacks six full-bleed banner images, each one a link, with the headline text baked into the image file. And it ships two different image files — a wide one for desktop, a taller one for phones.

ApproachCost
Text baked into image
(what Minimalist does)		Marketing can ship a new banner without a developer. But: search engines can't read it, screen readers can't read it, it can't be translated, and it gets blurry on high-density screens.
Real HTML text over an image
(what you should build)		Slower for marketing to change. But readable, searchable, accessible, and sharp at every screen size.

Both are real, defensible choices made by real teams. Learn the second one; recognise the first one. And notice the deeper point — the desktop and mobile banners are two separate files, not one image squeezed. Nobody solves mobile by shrinking.

Part two — margin and padding
02 / the box model

Padding is inside. Margin is outside. That's the whole thing.

This single distinction causes more beginner confusion than anything else in CSS, and it's genuinely simple once you see it drawn.

Three rules that stop 90% of spacing bugs

  • Margins collapse, padding never does. Two stacked boxes with margin-bottom: 20px and margin-top: 30px end up 30px apart, not 50px. CSS takes the larger. Padding always adds up.
  • Pick one direction and stay there. Space every element with margin-bottom only, or margin-top only — never both. Mixing them is what makes spacing feel unpredictable.
  • Between grid or flex children, don't use margin at all — use gap. One property, no collapse, no leftover margin on the last item.
/* the whole spacing system, four lines */ section { padding: 64px 24px; } /* air inside every section */ .grid { display: grid; gap: 24px; } /* air between the cards */ .card { padding: 16px; } /* air inside each card */ .card h3{ margin-bottom: 8px; } /* air under the card title */

The 8-point scale — stop inventing numbers

Every space in a professional interface comes from one short list: 4, 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96. Nothing else. You will never need 13px or 27px, and if you think you do, you're compensating for a different problem.

Part three — h1 to h6
03 / headings

Headings are an outline, not a size picker

The single biggest beginner mistake: choosing <h3> because you want smaller text. Headings describe structure. Size is CSS's problem, not HTML's.

The real audit — Minimalist's homepage, read live

Here is what's actually on their homepage, top to bottom. Read it as an outline and see whether it makes sense on its own:

h2Shop by CategoryCorrect.

h2The future of personal care is hereCorrect — and note it's a sentence, not a label. Brand voice lives in headings too.
h3Transparency · Efficacy · Affordable · Only the bestCorrectly nested under the h2 above. This is a textbook h2→h3 relationship.
h3Minimalist Trust Circle · Download Our AppAlso h3, but not conceptually inside the h2 above them. Debatable.

The uncomfortable finding — and the best discussion question in this notebook

Scanning that homepage, the heading outline starts at h2. There's a rotating banner carousel where the headline text is baked into image files — so the biggest, most important words on the page are pictures, not headings.

Ask the class: if the largest text on the homepage isn't a heading, what is a search engine supposed to think this page is about? There's a real answer either way — the brand name carries enormous search weight regardless, and marketing agility has genuine commercial value. But make them argue it, rather than repeat a rule.

The product page does it properly

Same site, different template, much cleaner outline — because a product page has one obvious subject:

<h1>Niacinamide 10% Face Serum</h1> <!-- the one subject --> <h2>Ingredients</h2> <h2>You might also like</h2> <h3>AI Generated Review Summary</h3> <h3>Review highlights</h3> <h4>Perfect for Sensitive Skin</h4> <!-- each review title --> <h2>FAQs</h2>

One h1, sensible h2s, reviews nested three deep. This is a good outline. Note the h1 is the short product name — while the browser tab title is a long, keyword-heavy sentence. Two different jobs, two different strings.

1
Section heading is centred and small — 20px, not 40px. It's a signpost, not a hero. The products are the point; the label just tells you where you are.not every heading needs to be big.
2
The badge sits at the very top-left corner, flush — zero margin. Badges are stickers; a stuck-on label with a gap around it reads as an accident.position:absolute, top:0, left:0. no padding on the parent side.
3
Under the name, a concern line — "Acne Marks, Acne Prone & Oily Skin". Not a description; a matching key. The shopper isn't buying niacinamide, they're buying a solution to a problem they already named.this one line is the smartest thing on their card.
4
Three numbers, three jobs: pay this (bold), don't pay this (struck, grey), you saved this (red). Exactly the pattern from Sheet 02's Zomato teardown, on a completely different site.patterns transfer. that's why we learn them.
5
Size selector inside the card — you can pick 30ml vs 60ml without opening the product. Fewer clicks to purchase, but the card gets taller and denser. A real trade-off, deliberately taken.most brands don't do this. ask the class why minimalist does.
6
Every card is the same height, even though the names are different lengths. That's min-height on the name and concern lines, plus display:flex; flex-direction:column so the button pins to the bottom.ragged card bottoms are the #1 giveaway of a beginner grid.
/* why the buttons all line up at the bottom */ .card { display: flex; flex-direction: column; } .card .name{ min-height: 33px; } /* two lines of room, always */ .card .atc { margin-top: auto; } /* eat the leftover space */
05 / sections & paragraphs

A section is a band. A paragraph has a maximum width.

<section class="bestsellers"> <div class="container"> <h2>Our Best Sellers</h2> <div class="grid"> ...cards... </div> </div> </section> .bestsellers { background: #f6f4f1; padding: 64px 0; } .container { max-width: 1200px; margin: 0 auto; padding: 0 24px; } p { max-width: 65ch; line-height: 1.6; }

1
Breadcrumb — three links and two slashes. The cheapest orientation device on the web, and the one beginners always leave out.Home / Category / This product. that's it.
2
The <h1> is the short name — "Niacinamide 10% Face Serum". The browser tab carries a much longer keyword sentence. Two different strings for two different readers: one for humans, one for search.<title> ≠ <h1>. most people never learn this.
3
Property chips — fragrance free, non-comedogenic, pH range. Tiny, bordered, no colour. They answer objections without interrupting the buying flow.notice they're facts, not adjectives. no "amazing", no "revolutionary".
4
Offers block, dashed border. A dashed border is the universal "coupon" signal — it's imitating a tear-off voucher. Real design references a physical object you already understand.border-style: dashed. one word, whole metaphor.
5
Variants are visible pills, not a dropdown, and the sold-out size stays on screen struck through rather than disappearing. Hiding it would make the shopper wonder if they misremembered.show unavailability. don't hide it.
6
The tax breakdown is spelled out: base ₹458 + 18% GST ₹82 = ₹540. Almost no global brand does this. It's Minimalist's transparency positioning, expressed as a spacing-and-type decision in the buy box.brand values become interface details. this is the clearest example i've found.
7
Pincode check + COD — the single most India-specific element on the page. Delivery certainty and cash-on-delivery are purchase blockers here in a way they aren't in the US or UK.geography changes the interface. always ask "where is the user".
8
Everything else is collapsed. Potency, ideal for, how to use, consumer studies — four accordions. This page holds an enormous amount of technical content; collapsing lets each shopper open only their own question.<details><summary> gives you this with zero javascript.
9
One large image, five thumbnails. Same gallery pattern as Sheet 00's KOSA page and as every e-commerce site you've ever used. Once you know a pattern, you stop having to invent one.this is the fourth time we've seen this exact component.

The block your students will never see on a Western site

Inside "FAQs" sits a specification table: product type, net quantity, shelf life, product dimensions, country of origin, SKU codes, and the full manufacturer and consumer-care address in Jaipur. That isn't marketing copy — most of it is legally required for goods sold online in India under Legal Metrology packaged-commodity rules.

This is the lesson: some parts of a page exist because the law says so, not because a designer wanted them. A student who builds a beautiful Indian e-commerce page with no country of origin and no consumer-care address has built something that couldn't legally ship. Worth one slide, and worth saying out loud.

Part six — this week's exercise
07 / do it

Measure first. Build second.

StepDoYou'll learn
1 · CountOpen any Indian D2C store. Right-click a section heading → Inspect. Write down its tag: is it h1, h2 or a styled div?That real sites are inconsistent, and rules are choices.
2 · OutlineList every heading on one page in order, indented by level. Read the list alone. Does it describe the page?What a screen reader hears.
3 · MeasureInspect one product card. Note its padding, and the gap between cards. Are they multiples of 8?That the 8-point scale is real and in use.
4 · RebuildBuild one section — heading + four cards — from your notes only. No tracing.Grid, gap, padding, and why cards need equal height.
5 · BreakSet every margin and padding to 0. Screenshot. Then set them all to 40px. Screenshot.That spacing is the design.

Step 5 is the grade. A page with perfect type, perfect colour and no spacing is unreadable. A page with plain type, no colour and correct spacing looks professional. Make them prove that to themselves once and they'll never forget it.
