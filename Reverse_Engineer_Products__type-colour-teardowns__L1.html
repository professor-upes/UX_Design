Field notes · product design · sheet 02

Teardowns: the theory, caught in the act

Three screens rebuilt from scratch, annotated where every type and colour decision from Sheet 01 shows up. Nothing here is a screenshot — each screen is a reconstruction, built with the real tokens so it can be pulled apart. Logos are deliberately absent; the point is the system, not the mark.

Numbers on the screen = notes beside it

Why reconstruct instead of screenshot

A screenshot lets students admire. A reconstruction forces a decision at every pixel — what size is that, what grey is that, why is the rating green when the brand is red. You cannot rebuild a screen without understanding it. This is the exercise; the annotations are just my answers to it.

1
Location header is the largest text on the screen at 16px — smaller than any headline you'd draw in class. Marketplace screens have no hero. The scale here runs 16 / 15.5 / 12.5 / 12 / 11: five steps inside a five-pixel range. density is the aesthetic. tight ratio, ~1.12.
2
Search placeholder is grey on a near-white field. This is the most common contrast failure in Indian D2C and it appears in the real product too — placeholder copy that carries actual information ("cuisine or dish") should not sit below 4.5:1. flag it. teach students to notice it in shipped apps, not just their own.
3
The rating pill is green, on a red-branded app. Green is spent on quality/safety — a semantic job — so it can never be confused with promotion. Sheet 01, note 11: brand colour and semantic colour must not overlap. the one colour students always get wrong.
4
ETA and rating both use tabular figures (font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums). Proportional digits make "28 min" and "41 min" different widths, and the column stops scanning cleanly. one CSS line. enormous difference down a long list.
5
Red appears exactly once per card, on the offer line, below a dashed divider. Card one has it; card two doesn't. That asymmetry is the whole point — the red is a signal, and a signal that appears everywhere is wallpaper. count the red pixels. it's under 4% of the screen.
6
The food image is the only saturated area, so the surface behind it is a warm off-white #F4F4F2 rather than pure white. Pure white would make the photography look dirty. neutrals are chosen to flatter the content, not to be invisible.
7
White on #E23744 is roughly 3.9:1 — a fail for body text, a pass for large. It survives here only because the label is 15px semibold and sits on a full-bleed bar. Shrink this button and it becomes non-compliant. the brand colour dictates the button size. not the other way round.

1
Artwork gets 290 of 706 pixels — over 40% of the screen, and it is the only saturated region. Everything else is greyscale by design. The UI has voluntarily disarmed so a stranger's album cover can win. this is the strategy in one number.
2
Title 22/700, artist 14/400 in #B3B3B3. Hierarchy built from size + weight + one grey — three dials, zero colour spent. Sheet 01, note 05: colour is the dial you save for last. no accent needed. the gap does the work.
3
The progress track is #4D4D4D against a #121212 ground — about 3.1:1, just clearing the 3:1 bar for a non-text UI component. It is deliberately near the floor so the filled white portion reads instantly. low contrast can be correct. it has to be measured, not guessed.
4
The play triangle is black on green, not white. White on #1ED760 is about 1.7:1 — unusable. Black is roughly 11.6:1. The maths made this call before any designer did. the single best example i know of a constraint producing an identity.
5
The lyrics panel is #1F1F1F — lighter than the page, not shadowed. In dark mode, elevation is expressed by lightness, because shadow is invisible on a dark ground. the rule students always miss when they "add dark mode".
6
Green appears on exactly one element. Everything else is white, grey or black. Same 4% discipline as Zomato, arrived at from the opposite direction. two very different brands. identical restraint.

The dark-surface argument, stated properly

Students assume dark mode is a mood or a battery decision. Here it is neither. Album art arrives in every hue, saturation and crop imaginable. On white, a dark cover looks like a hole and a pale cover dissolves. On near-black, every cover reads as deliberate. The dark canvas is a framing device for content the company cannot art-direct.

1
The sidebar is #F7F7F5 and the text is #37352F — both warm. Notion's entire grey ramp is ecru-tinted rather than the cool blue-grey most tech products default to. Even the shadows are warm-tinted so they stay in chromatic agreement. warm vs cool greys. nobody notices. everybody feels it.
2
H1 at 27px against 12.5px body is a ratio of about 2.1 — a much wider jump than Zomato's 1.12. A page has one subject; a marketplace has a hundred. The scale follows the job. same alphabet. opposite answer. this is the whole course.
3
Body sits at line-height 1.66 — generous, because this is a reading surface, not a scanning surface. Compare Zomato's card meta at ~1.35. Leading is a statement about how long you expect someone to stay. reading vs scanning. set the leading accordingly.
4
The cover gradient and the compass emoji are user-supplied. Strip them and this screen is pure greyscale. Notion designed a system that is deliberately incomplete without you. the boldest colour decision here is to not make one.
5
The single accent, #0075DE, appears only on a link — held closer to a hyperlink hue than a brand accent. It is doing a functional job, not an identity one. one blue. one job. that's the entire palette.
6
Status tags use very pale semantic backgrounds with dark text — an editorial green and a warm amber rather than saturated chips. This keeps them inside the warm-grey system instead of tearing a hole in it. semantic colour can be quiet and still be instant.

Method — run this yourself
05 / the exercise

Rebuild, don't redesign

Sheet 01 warned against the unsolicited-redesign portfolio piece. This is the replacement, and it teaches far more.

StepDoProduces
1 · CaptureOne screen from a real app. Not the home page — a screen where a decision happens.A constrained target.
2 · MeasureSample every colour and estimate every size. Write them in a table before opening a design tool.The shock: usually 9+ greys and 7+ sizes.
3 · RebuildReconstruct from the table only, never by tracing. HTML/CSS or Figma both fine.Every gap in their understanding, exposed.
4 · AnnotateNumber five decisions and write one line each on why, not what.The actual deliverable.
5 · Break itChange one token. Screenshot the damage. Explain what stopped working.Proof they understood the system, not the surface.

Marking: step 5 is where the grade lives. A student who changes the rating pill to red and writes "now the trust signal reads as a promotion" has understood product design. A student who submits a beautiful rebuild with no step 5 has submitted a drawing.

Two honest caveats to say out loud

  • These reconstructions are built from observation, not from any company's published specs. Sizes and greys are close, not exact. Say so — the method is the lesson, and a student who later measures a real value and finds it differs has done the exercise correctly.
  • Rebuilding for study is fine. Shipping a rebuilt interface, or presenting one as your own product design, is not. Draw that line explicitly before the assignment, not after.
